# Resume Writing Playbook

Use this guide when drafting or rewriting resume content. Preserve the candidate's meaning and evidence while increasing relevance and clarity.

## Give each section one job

- **Header:** Identify the candidate and provide current, professional contact details. Add a target title only when supported by the resume.
- **Profile:** Summarize role fit, domain scope, and strongest verified differentiators in two to four lines. Do not repeat a list of tools or every experience.
- **Experience:** Demonstrate contribution and impact in reverse chronological order. Prioritize evidence relevant to the target role.
- **Projects or research:** Show applied methods, ownership, and outcomes not already covered by experience.
- **Education:** State institution, qualification, field, and dates. Retain coursework only when it adds target-role evidence.
- **Skills and extras:** Group verifiable tools, languages, certifications, awards, or activities. Do not use this section to introduce unsupported proficiency claims.

## Build evidence-led bullets

Prefer this structure:

`Action + object or scope + method or tool + result`

Apply it as follows:

1. Start with a precise action verb that reflects the candidate's role.
2. Name what changed, was built, analyzed, delivered, or influenced.
3. Add scale, stakeholders, constraints, or method when verified and useful.
4. End with a result, decision, efficiency gain, quality improvement, or other observable outcome.

Do not force every component into every bullet. Keep one main claim per bullet and lead with the most relevant evidence. Replace duty-only wording with contribution wording, but do not imply ownership the candidate did not have.

## Preserve evidence integrity

- Use numbers, rankings, scope, causality, and business outcomes only when present in the source resume or confirmed by the user.
- Never estimate or invent a metric to make a bullet sound stronger.
- When no number exists, state a specific qualitative outcome, deliverable, decision, audience, or process improvement that is supported by the source.
- Mark potentially valuable missing facts as questions for the user; do not insert them into the resume as facts.
- Preserve employer, role, degree, date, publication, award, certification, and tool facts unless the user corrects them.

## Keep language readable

- Use present tense for ongoing work and past tense for completed work.
- Keep tense and grammatical structure parallel within an entry.
- Omit first-person pronouns and filler such as “responsible for.”
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over vague claims such as “excellent,” “various,” or “successfully.”
- Use technical terminology when it is accurate and relevant; translate niche jargon into language a non-specialist recruiter can understand.
- Define an uncommon acronym on first use unless the JD treats it as standard.
- Read each bullet once for meaning and once for skimmability. Split or trim sentences that carry multiple unrelated claims.

## Final content check

Confirm that every claim is traceable to candidate evidence, each section has a distinct purpose, the strongest JD-relevant evidence appears early, and no rewrite changes seniority, ownership, or causality.
